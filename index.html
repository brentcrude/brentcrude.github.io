About

Fewer moving parts, more finished work.

Most of what I write starts as a personal annoyance and ends as something self-contained. A humming-to-sheet-music tool that runs entirely in the browser. A browsing filter that deliberately has no server, because browsing history should not live on somebody else's computer. A document signer that is one HTML file.

The through-line is local-first: keep the data where the user is, keep the dependency list boring, and make the thing actually finish. I care more about a tool that gets used than an architecture that looks impressive in a diagram.

NisSign — sign a PDF that never leaves your machine

Drop in a PDF, place your signature, initials and date fields, draw on the pad, and export a signed document. It runs entirely in your browser — there is no upload step, because there is no server behind it at all.

  • Free, with nothing withheld. No account, no sign-up, no trial, no watermark on the export, no paid tier waiting at the end.
  • Nothing is sent to a server. The PDF you open, every stroke you draw and the file you export stay on your own device. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to.
  • Private by architecture, not by promise. No backend means no document store to breach and no audit log of what you signed. Close the tab and it is gone.

Local by design

  • CostFree
  • Sign-upNot required
  • Uploads to a serverNone
  • Document stored elsewhereNever
  • Tracking or analyticsNone
  • Close the tabEverything wiped

The PDF engine loads from a public CDN when you first open the tool; your document is never part of that request. NisSign is a local signing tool with an honest integrity fingerprint — not a certificate-backed e-signature. It says so itself, in its own security notes.
