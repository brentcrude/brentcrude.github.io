    Security & privacy notes

    Everything happens in your browser. The PDF you load, every stroke you draw, and the file you export never touch a server. Closing the tab clears everything.

    How re-importing works. When you export, NisSign writes a small, plain-text field manifest (positions, types, labels, who typed what, when) into the PDF's own Subject metadata field, alongside a SHA-256 fingerprint of the file. When anyone — including a different signer on a different computer — loads that PDF back into NisSign, it reads that manifest and automatically reconstructs the layout: already-completed fields show up locked (their ink is already burned into the page), and any still-empty fields you left for someone else show up ready to fill. This is how a contractor can sign, email the PDF to a client, and have the client's copy of NisSign already know where the client's signature field goes.

    This metadata is plain, not hidden. Anyone can see it in the file's Properties/Subject field — NisSign doesn't disguise it. Stripping metadata (e.g. with a "flatten/optimize PDF" tool) just turns off auto-detection on the next import; it doesn't change any signature already drawn on the page.

    What this is not. Not a legally-binding e-signature under ESIGN, UETA, or eIDAS. No identity verification, no PKI-based cryptographic signature Acrobat could validate against a certificate authority, no trusted third-party timestamp (the clock is whatever the signer's device says), no server-side audit log. The fingerprint chain (original → each hop → final) is an honest integrity trail for good-faith parties, not tamper-proof against someone determined to forge it.
